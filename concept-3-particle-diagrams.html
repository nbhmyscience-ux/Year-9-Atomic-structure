CONCEPT 3

Particle diagrams

LEARNING OBJECTIVE
Represent elements, compounds and mixtures using particle diagrams, and tell them apart.
BIG IDEA
Dalton drew atoms as simple spheres. Whether those spheres are all one type, joined in a fixed ratio, or just mixed together loose tells you whether you're looking at an element, a compound, or a mixture.

THE BIG QUESTION

Dalton drew atoms as simple spheres. If that's all we've got to work with, how do we draw the difference between an element, a compound, and a mixture?

KW: A molecule is two or more non-metal atoms joined together. This can be the same element (like O₂) or different elements (like H₂O).
KW: A compound is a new substance made when two or more different atoms are chemically combined, always in a fixed ratio.
KW: A mixture is two or more substances put together but not chemically joined.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS

✕ If two different atoms are drawn near each other, that makes them a compound — they only count as a compound if they're actually joined.
✕ Carbon monoxide (CO) and carbon dioxide (CO₂) must be the same substance since they contain the same two elements — a different fixed ratio makes a completely different substance.
