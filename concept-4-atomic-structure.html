CONCEPT 4

Atomic structure

LEARNING OBJECTIVE
Describe the location, relative charge and relative mass of the proton, neutron and electron, and explain why an atom has no overall charge.
BIG IDEA
An atom isn't uncuttable after all — it's built from three even smaller particles. Where they sit and what charge they carry explains why atoms are neutral overall.

THE BIG QUESTION

Back in Lesson 1, you found the atom was the smallest piece of gold that could exist. Let's cut it open anyway.

What do you think we'd find inside — is it really empty, or is something hiding in there?

THE REVEAL · THREE PARTICLES INSIDE EVERY ATOM

If an atom were the size of a football stadium, the nucleus would be a single pea at the centre — and that tiny pea would hold almost all of the atom's mass.
EVERY ATOM OF A GIVEN ELEMENT SHARES THIS
Every atom of the same element has the same number of protons
KW: The nucleus is the tiny, dense centre of an atom, made of protons and neutrons.
KW: A neutral atom has equal numbers of protons and electrons, so the charges cancel out exactly.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS

✕ Electrons live inside the nucleus — they don't, only protons and neutrons do.
✕ Mass is spread evenly through the atom — almost all of it is packed into the tiny nucleus.
